A fraud model can have an excellent ROC-AUC and still be a poor production system.

Fraud modelling is constrained by much more than predictive performance. Data availability, leakage, temporal drift, review capacity, latency, feature stability and business cost all influence whether a model is actually useful.

The central idea

Let the agent reason about the problem. Let deterministic code enforce the rules.

03

Point-in-time correctness

Suppose a transaction occurs at T0. Every feature used by the model must represent information that genuinely existed at that moment.

T0 · SCORING

Information available at decision time

Transaction amount, device information, account age and historical behaviour may be valid inputs.

T+7 · INVESTIGATION

New information begins to appear

Investigation processes can generate information that did not exist at T0.

T+14 · CONFIRMED FRAUD

Predictive — but unavailable at T0

Investigation outcomes may be highly predictive, but using them as T0 features creates temporal leakage.

Important distinction

Predictive power does not make a feature valid. Availability at the scoring timestamp does.

04

Feature selection is a sequence of gates

With hundreds or thousands of candidate features, simply ranking everything by feature importance is not enough.

Availability

Can the feature actually be computed when the fraud decision is made?

Candidate features ↓ Availability gate ↓ Point-in-time + leakage gate ↓ Stability + redundancy ↓ Model-based importance ↓ Selected features
05

ROC-AUC vs Catch@1%

Imagine a fraud operations team can manually investigate only the highest-risk 1% of transactions.

The question is no longer simply:

"How well does the model rank transactions overall?"

It becomes:

"How much fraud can we capture inside the 1% we can actually review?"

ROC-AUC

ROC-AUC evaluates how well the model ranks positive examples above negative examples across thresholds.

It is useful for measuring general discrimination, but it does not directly encode a fixed investigation capacity.

Why TPE?

Random search does not learn much from previous experiments. Bayesian optimisation methods such as Tree-structured Parzen Estimation use trial history to guide subsequent experiments.

11

The bigger lesson

The interesting part of an agentic fraud modelling system isn't simply adding an LLM to an AutoML pipeline.

The interesting part is connecting business intent, temporal correctness, feature eligibility, model search and operational evaluation into one reproducible workflow.

LLMs Reason
Deterministic pipelines Enforce
Optimisers Search
Temporal validation Challenge
Business metrics Define success

The model is still important. But the larger engineering challenge is building a system that can reliably decide what model should be built, what information it is allowed to see, how it should be evaluated, and whether its performance actually matters to the business.
