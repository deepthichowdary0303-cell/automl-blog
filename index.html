03

Point-in-time correctness

Suppose a transaction occurs at T0. Every feature used by the model must represent information that genuinely existed at that moment.

T0 · SCORING

Information available at decision time

Transaction amount, device information, account age and historical behaviour may be valid inputs.

T+7 · INVESTIGATION

New information begins to appear

Investigation processes can generate information that did not exist at T0.

T+14 · CONFIRMED FRAUD

Predictive — but unavailable at T0

Confirmed-fraud information may be highly predictive, but using it at T0 creates temporal leakage.

Rule:

Predictive power does not make a feature valid. Availability at the scoring timestamp does.

07

Why TPE?

When every model evaluation is expensive, the goal isn't to try everything. It's to spend the experimentation budget where it is most useful.

Imagine a search space containing 1,000 possible configurations. Each configuration can combine a model family, feature subset and hyperparameters. Training every possibility may be unnecessary.

RANDOM SEARCH

Sample independently

Configurations are sampled according to the search strategy without using the performance history to concentrate future trials.

TPE

Learn from trial history

TPE uses previous observations to estimate which parameter regions are more promising, then favours useful areas while still exploring.

Experimental results

Architecture explains how the system works. Experiments demonstrate whether it actually improves the modelling process.

What does the business objective mean?

In fraud detection, the model may score thousands or millions of transactions, but the fraud operations team typically has a limited capacity for manual investigation.

If investigators can review only the top 1% of transactions ranked by fraud risk, the relevant business question is:

How much of the actual fraud can we capture within that top 1%?

This is what Catch@1% measures. It is the percentage of all actual fraud cases that appear within the highest-risk 1% of transactions.

Catch@1% = Fraud cases captured in the top 1% ÷ Total fraud cases × 100

For example, if a test period contains 500 actual fraud cases and 213 of them fall within the highest-risk 1% of transactions:

Catch@1% = 213 ÷ 500 = 42.6%

Therefore, the 42.6% result in the Business Objective experiment means that 42.6% of the fraud cases were captured within the top 1% of transactions selected for review.

What do the experiments show?

The Default AutoML experiment achieves a ROC-AUC of 0.842 using random validation. When the evaluation is changed to a temporal split, the ROC-AUC falls to 0.801. This highlights why temporal validation is important when future transactions may differ from historical data.

The next experiment changes the optimisation objective from general ranking quality to the operational objective of Catch@1%. This establishes a business-aligned baseline of 42.6%.

CASH + TPE then searches across model configurations and hyperparameters while using information from previous trials to guide subsequent experiments. Catch@1% increases from 42.6% to 48.9%.

Finally, the full pipeline combines the modelling search with the earlier data and feature controls, including temporal validation, feature eligibility and leakage-aware selection. Catch@1% reaches 53.7%.

The key takeaway

Catch@1% improves from 42.6% → 48.9% → 53.7% as the pipeline moves from a business-aligned baseline to intelligent model search and then to the full data-and-feature-aware workflow. In other words, the final pipeline captures more fraud within the same fixed review budget.

The bigger lesson

The interesting part of an agentic fraud modelling system isn't simply adding an LLM to an AutoML pipeline.

It is connecting business intent, temporal correctness, feature eligibility, model search and operational evaluation into one reproducible workflow.

The model is still important. But the larger engineering challenge is building a system that can reliably decide what model should be built, what information it is allowed to see, how it should be evaluated, and whether its performance actually matters to the business.
